# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This folder contains the core project management guidance used by OctoAcme for planning, delivery, quality, release, and continuous improvement. These documents provide a shared operating model for cross-functional teams so work stays aligned from idea to implementation and post-release learning.

## Project Management Process Overview

OctoAcme runs projects through a simple lifecycle: initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective improvement. During initiation, the team validates the business need, identifies stakeholders and success metrics, and decides whether to move forward. Planning translates an approved initiative into a prioritized backlog, milestone map, risk register, and Definition of Done so the team can execute with clear scope and ownership.

During execution, teams work in short iterative increments, track progress on project boards, and rely on regular status, review, and standup rhythms. Project managers coordinate schedules and dependencies, product managers prioritize outcomes, developers build and validate features, and QA ensures acceptance criteria and quality standards are met. Workflows emphasize small PRs, issue linkage, CI validation, and defined review and approval practices to reduce risk and keep teams aligned.

Communication is built around stakeholder clarity and follow-through. OctoAcme documents regular status updates, weekly planning or PM syncs, demos, and escalation paths for blockers or business-impacting issues. The project team uses one source of truth for progress, risks, and decisions, while incident communication and stakeholder reporting help keep everyone informed when issues arise. Quality and operational controls are built into the workflow through automated testing, security scans, smoke tests, and manual QA when needed.

Release and post-release activities close the loop by verifying deployment readiness, documenting release notes, and confirming rollback or mitigation plans if incidents occur. After each sprint, release, or milestone, the team uses retrospectives to capture what went well, what should improve, and what actions must be tracked. This continuous-improvement cycle helps OctoAcme adapt quickly while preserving a consistent, repeatable process.
